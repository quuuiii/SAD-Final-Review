1. Core Concepts & Terminology

Requirement: A statement of what a system must do or what characteristic it must have to serve and support users' needs. Requirements Determination: Gathering, analyzing, refining and documenting needed capabilities and constraints.

本章屬於 SDLC Analysis。它把 System Request 中的高階 Business Need 細化成清楚的 Requirements Definition，支援後續 Functional、Structural、Behavioral Models，最後整合為 System Proposal。需求錯誤越晚發現，返工成本越大，因此不能把蒐集到的片段敘述直接當作已確認規格。

Functional vs Non-functional Requirements

Functional: What business processes the system must perform or information it must manage. Nonfunctional: Behavioral properties, qualities or constraints under which it operates.

CategoryDefinitionExample / 中文判斷
FunctionalSystem process or information capabilityGenerate daily appointment schedules（產生報表）
OperationalOperating environment or technology constraintSupport wireless printing（操作環境）
PerformanceSpeed, capacity, reliability and availabilityRetrieve a schedule within 2 seconds（效能）
SecurityAccess and data protectionOnly doctors can update availability（權限）
Cultural & PoliticalRegulatory and organizational constraintsComply with applicable privacy regulation（法規）

混合句型如「Doctors may retrieve medical records within two seconds」應視品質管理需要拆為 Functional Capability 和 measurable Performance Requirement。

2. Eight Requirements Analysis Strategies

Strategies help discover problems, causes and opportunities in business processes; they are different from the five Gathering Techniques used to obtain evidence.

StrategyCore QuestionBest Use
1. Problem AnalysisWhat current problems exist?User pain points and defects.
2. Root Cause AnalysisWhy is the problem occurring?Repeated symptoms; uncover root causes.
3. Duration AnalysisWhere is elapsed time spent?Process delays and handoffs.
4. Activity-Based CostingWhich activities cost the most?Business process cost reduction.
5. Informal BenchmarkingHow do comparable organizations succeed?Learn external practices.
6. Outcome AnalysisWhat end result is desired?Result-first process redesign.
7. Technology AnalysisCan technology enable new capabilities?Technology-enabled business change.
8. Activity EliminationWhich step can be removed?Simplify or redesign process.

2.1 Problem Analysis

Definition: Ask users what difficulties and inefficiencies they experience, then clarify concrete examples, frequency, impact and current workarounds. SOP: Identify Stakeholders → Ask Problems → Probe Specific Occurrences → Record Business Impact → Verify Issue → Form Improvement Hypothesis.

如果接待員說「預約常常重複」，不代表解決方案必然是換畫面。先問何時重複、由誰記錄、什麼情況會出錯。

2.2 Root Cause Analysis

Definition: Investigate underlying causes instead of treating only symptoms. Typical artifact: Fishbone / Cause-and-Effect Diagram.

flowchart LR\n A["Incorrect Inventory Data"] --> P["Inventory Stock-Outs"]\n B["Delayed Purchase Approval"] --> P\n C["Wrong Reorder Threshold"] --> P\n D["Slow Supplier Delivery"] --> P\n E["Unrecorded Sales"] --> P
Copyable Mermaid diagram source
flowchart LR\n A["Incorrect Inventory Data"] --> P["Inventory Stock-Outs"]\n B["Delayed Purchase Approval"] --> P\n C["Wrong Reorder Threshold"] --> P\n D["Slow Supplier Delivery"] --> P\n E["Unrecorded Sales"] --> P

此 Mermaid 為因果關係示意；教授的 Fishbone Diagram 應用魚骨布局。SOP: Precisely define effect → Brainstorm categories → Decompose contributing causes → Validate evidence → Derive corrective requirements. 錯誤示範：只在缺貨後增加通知，卻不檢查庫存更新不正確的根本原因。

2.3 Duration Analysis

Definition: Compare actual working time required by process activities with the entire elapsed process duration. Investigate large gaps caused by waiting, handoffs, or redundant steps.

ActivityProcessing (min)Elapsed (min)
Receive request33
Wait for review035
Verify booking44
Wait for confirmation018
Send confirmation22
Total962

62 minutes end-to-end minus 9 minutes processing = 53 minutes waiting. Possible responses: eliminate steps, integrate steps, or execute independent tasks in parallel. 等待不必然等於浪費；如果有法定審核，需先確認是否能取消。

2.4 Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

Definition: Analyze costs assigned to activities to identify major cost drivers and improvement opportunities. Compare the cost of manual data entry, calls, corrections and paper processing with prospective automation and support costs.

ActivityIllustrative Monthly Cost
Manual booking entryNT$12,000
Confirmation callsNT$8,000
Error correctionNT$6,000
PaperworkNT$2,000
TotalNT$28,000

ABC focuses the allocation and consequences of costs by business activity, not only the initial total software project budget. 數字是案例示例，不是課本提供的診所真實數據。

2.5 Informal Benchmarking

Definition: Compare successful organizations' processes to identify ideas, then adapt them to local circumstances. SOP: Select comparable process → Study its practices → Identify gap → Evaluate organizational fit → Adapt. 注意 Benchmarking 不代表完全抄襲其他組織的規則。

2.6 Outcome Analysis

Definition: Begin with the desired business outcome and derive suitable process changes and requirements. Lecturer's example: reducing abnormal mass ticket cancellations, examining scalping and identity controls.

flowchart TD\n G["Desired Outcome: Fewer abnormal cancellations"] --> C["Investigate causes"]\n C --> M["Evaluate methods: identity verification"]\n M --> E["Derive requirements and controls"]

flowchart TD\n G["Desired Outcome: Fewer abnormal cancellations"] --> C["Investigate causes"]\n C --> M["Evaluate methods: identity verification"]\n M --> E["Derive requirements and controls"]

不應因為想用 Facial Recognition 就直接認定那是唯一需求；還要評估 privacy, misidentification, security and regulations。

2.7 Technology Analysis

Definition: Assess available or emerging technology for business opportunities. SOP: Discover capability → Determine business use → Evaluate feasibility and risks → Derive requirements → Confirm stakeholder value. Example: Automatic reminders → reduce no-shows → notification schedule requirement. Technology is a means to business value.

2.8 Activity Elimination

Definition: Challenge the necessity of existing activities, often through a Force-Fit Exercise: assume the step must be removed and redesign the remaining process.

flowchart LR\n A["Patient Form"] --> B["Manual Re-entry"] --> C["Schedule Check"] --> D["Approval"] --> E["Confirmation"]

flowchart LR\n A["Patient Form"] --> B["Manual Re-entry"] --> C["Schedule Check"] --> D["Approval"] --> E["Confirmation"]

Ask whether manual re-entry and some approvals are truly required. Cannot eliminate legally necessary, clinically necessary, or security-sensitive steps purely for speed.

3. Five Requirements Gathering Techniques

TechniqueStrengthLimit / BiasBest Context
InterviewsDetail, clarification and follow-upTime-consuming; interviewer effectsExplore complicated business rules
JADShared decisions and conflict resolutionScheduling and facilitation overheadMultiple stakeholders need consensus
QuestionnairesScale, standardized answersLimited depth, nonresponse biasLarge distributed user base
ObservationReveal real workaroundsObserver effect; periodic work missedActual operations differ from documented process
Document AnalysisRecords existing forms and rulesMay be obsolete or inaccurateStudy policies, forms, reports and records

3.1 Interviews

Definition: Direct conversations with stakeholders to elicit needs, problems and business facts. Structured interviews use predetermined questions; Unstructured interviews follow responses; semi-structured interviews blend both (a general methodological supplement). Question Types: Open-ended, Closed-ended and Probing.

Question TypeExampleBest Purpose
Open-endedWhat difficulties arise during booking?Explore unknown issues
Closed-endedHow many bookings are processed daily?Obtain precise/comparable facts
ProbingWhy is manager approval required?Clarify reasons and exceptions

Top-Down: General business objectives → Specific process questions. Bottom-Up: Specific operating details → General conclusions. SOP: Select Interviewees → Prepare Questions → Interview and record → Probe → Summarize → Confirm notes with participants → Update requirements. Interview notes must be verified, not treated as final facts.

3.2 Questionnaires

Definition: Standardized written questions completed independently by respondents. Good for geographically distributed stakeholders and quantitative comparisons. Use neutral questions; avoid double-barreled questions; pilot-test; choose consistent response scales and prepare an analysis strategy. Low response or misunderstanding can threaten validity.

3.3 Observation

Definition: Systematically examine actual work activities and behavior. Detect undocumented shortcuts and activities users forget to mention. Major threats: Hawthorne / Observer Effect and missing infrequent or periodic tasks. SOP: Define observed task → Obtain permission → Observe with minimal interference → Record actions, delays and exceptions → Check weekly/monthly tasks → Validate with staff.

3.4 Document Analysis

Definition: Examine existing forms, policies, reports, user manuals and records for information about current business operations. Pay attention to user-added fields (missing information requirements) and unused fields (possibly obsolete requirements). Confirm tentative findings with users. For example, handwritten Preferred Contact Method suggests a candidate data requirement, not an automatically approved one.

3.5 Joint Application Development (JAD)

Definition: Structured requirements workshops involving users, analysts, managers and facilitators. Roles may include Facilitator, Users, Analysts, Decision Makers and Scribe. SOP: Define goals → Select participants → Prepare agenda/material → Agree ground rules → Facilitate discussion → Record issues/decisions → Confirm outputs. Success depends on knowledgeable participation and effective facilitation.

4. Requirements Documentation, Prioritization & Scope

Two Requirements Formats

Descriptive Requirement Statement: FR-014: The system shall allow authorized receptionists to reschedule an existing appointment when the requested slot is available.

User Story: As a receptionist, I want to reschedule appointments so that patient changes can be accommodated without conflicting with doctor availability.

DimensionDescriptive StatementUser Story
StructureActor/system + action + object + conditionAs a [role], I want [capability], so that [value]
StrengthExplicit testable specificationStakeholder goal/value
LimitationMay become overtechnicalNeeds further acceptance criteria and exceptions

Story Cards are lightweight artifacts for maintaining stories; a story alone rarely captures all security, exception and performance conditions.

MoSCoW and Dependency Ordering

PriorityMeaningExample
Must HaveEssential to the agreed scopePrevent double-booking
Should HaveHigh value, but not blocking first releaseAppointment reminders
Could HaveDesirable enhancementPersonalization theme
Won't Have (for now)Explicitly excluded from current scopeIntegrated insurance claim processing

Priority (business importance) and Sequence (technical/business dependencies) are separate. Example: Register Patient → Schedule Appointment → Send Confirmation, if registration is required before scheduling. Confirm that assumption with stakeholders.

Formal Four-Step Requirements Definition Process

  1. Gather and Analyze a set of Requirements.
  2. Document Requirements.
  3. Verify and Prioritize them.
  4. Repeat for further requirements or necessary refinement, while controlling scope.

5. Five Text Analysis Tools & VT4C Quality Assurance

5.1 Sentence Diagramming

Analyze sentence components to identify candidate Objects, Actions and Relationships: The patient contacts the office regarding an appointment. Patient (role/object) → contacts (action) → Office (participant); Appointment (relevant business object). A noun is only a candidate Class, never automatically a final Class.

5.2 Domain Analysis

Identify domain boundaries and multiple meanings of concepts. Example: Student consultation may include Thesis Advising, Course Advising, Personal Counseling and Career Consultation. Domain Analysis reduces omitted scenarios and false assumptions.

5.3 Component Analysis

Identify relevant Attributes of business concepts: Patient may require patientId, name, phone, email and preferred contact method. Do not collect attributes irrelevant to the agreed business scope.

5.4 Taxonomic Analysis

Identify classification structures and concept relationships, including kinds of consultations. Not every conceptual category hierarchy should be converted uncritically into UML inheritance.

flowchart TD\n C["Consultation"] --> A["Academic Consultation"]\n C --> P["Personal Consultation"]\n C --> R["Career Consultation"]\n A --> T["Thesis Advising"]\n A --> U["Course Advising"]
Copyable Mermaid diagram source
flowchart TD\n C["Consultation"] --> A["Academic Consultation"]\n C --> P["Personal Consultation"]\n C --> R["Career Consultation"]\n A --> T["Thesis Advising"]\n A --> U["Course Advising"]

5.5 Theme Analysis

Find additional and exceptional scenarios that may be omitted by the main workflow. Ticket purchase example: family members want adjacent seats while anti-scalping rules still apply. Theme Analysis concerns business patterns, not interface color themes.

VT4C Principle (Six Criteria)

CriterionEnglish DefinitionTypical Quality Failure
VerifiableObjectively testable or evaluableThe system shall be beautiful.
TraceableLinkable by ID to source, models and testsNo requirement ID or origin
ClearUnambiguous interpretationThe system shall be fast.
ConsistentNo contradictory requirementsGuests both allowed and prohibited under same conditions
CompleteRequired actor, rules and exceptions describedThe system shall manage appointments
ConciseFocused and free of unnecessary wordingLong promotional prose without definite behavior

VT4C Revision Examples

OriginalIssueImproved Requirement
The system should be easy to use.Unclear, unverifiableAt least 90% of representative users shall complete booking without assistance in an agreed usability test.
The system should provide beautiful reports.UnverifiableReports shall conform to an approved layout with title, period, headings and totals.
The system shall manage appointments.IncompleteSpecify create, view, reschedule and cancel privileges plus conflict rules.

Send a confirmation.Not traceable / incompleteFR-015: After accepted booking, send confirmation via the patient's selected channel.

Criteria labels are completed in accordance with the 2026 lecture's VT4C framework. Text Analysis and VT4C do different jobs: discovering meaning versus checking documented requirement quality.

6. Step-by-Step Modeling SOP & Cross-Model V&V

The lecturer's formal Requirements Definition Cycle contains FOUR steps. The following nine-step guide is an expanded classroom execution SOP, NOT a replacement for those four steps.

  1. Establish Business Context, Goals, Stakeholders and Scope.
  2. Investigate the As-Is Process using appropriate Analysis Strategies.
  3. Select Gathering Techniques based on stakeholder and evidence needs.
  4. Collect Statements, Observations, Documents and Exceptions.
  5. Apply Sentence, Domain, Component, Taxonomic and Theme Analysis.
  6. Write Descriptive Requirements and User Stories.
  7. Apply VT4C and confirm findings with stakeholders.
  8. Prioritize using dependencies and MoSCoW; baseline scope.
  9. Integrate Requirements Definition into the System Proposal and iterate.

Verification vs Validation

QuestionVerificationValidation
What is examined?Specification correctness and internal consistencyReal stakeholder/business needs
ExampleCan an unambiguous test be written?Will the capability actually solve the business problem?
EvidenceID, clarity, consistency, completenessStakeholder approval, scenario walkthrough, outcome check

Requirements Walkthrough SOP

  1. Select an Actor and representative scenario.
  2. Identify corresponding requirements.
  3. Simulate the workflow from the actor's viewpoint.
  4. Check information, roles, business rules and exceptions.
  5. Review the expected outcome with stakeholders.
  6. Record gaps and contradictions, revise and recheck.

Five Common Pitfalls

  1. Treating a symptom as the root cause.
  2. Turning all nouns directly into classes without domain/scope analysis.
  3. Writing vague requirements using fast, easy, attractive without criteria.
  4. Confusing manual human activities with automated system behavior.
  5. Ignoring unavailable slots, failed payments, error conditions and other exceptions.

7. Clinic Appointment System: End-to-End Walkthrough

Scenario Requirements (English)

A community health clinic currently manages appointments through telephone calls and paper-based schedules. Receptionists receive requests, check physician availability, and manually confirm booking times. Because multiple staff members update different records, appointment conflicts occasionally occur. Patients complain about delayed confirmations and long waiting times. The clinic wants an online appointment system that allows patients to search available consultation periods, request appointments, view bookings, and cancel or reschedule visits. Doctors should maintain available consultation periods, while receptionists assist those who prefer telephone services. The system must prevent double bookings and confirm accepted requests. Clinic managers want to reduce administrative workload without compromising patient information security. Before system design begins, the development team must analyze existing business problems, observe the current scheduling workflow, interview stakeholders, identify exceptional cases and document functional as well as non-functional requirements that can be verified and validated.

  2. FR-02: The system shall allow appointment requests for available periods.

  4. FR-04: The system shall allow patients to view existing bookings.

  • NFR-01 (Performance): Return availability search results within 2 seconds for at least 95% of requests under agreed workload.
  • NFR-02 (Security): Permit only authenticated authorized users to access patient appointment data.
  • NFR-03 (Operational): Support agreed desktop/mobile web browsers.
  • NFR-04 (Performance): Maintain agreed uptime during clinic operating hours. Target remains unresolved.
  • NFR-05 (Cultural/Political): Meet applicable privacy and patient protection laws. Specific applicable regulations remain unresolved.

9. Cheat Sheet & Eight Golden Rules

  1. Functional Requirements specify WHAT the system does; Non-functional Requirements specify qualities and constraints.
  2. Begin with real Business Problems, not a predetermined technology.
  3. Analysis Strategies identify improvements; Gathering Techniques collect evidence.
  4. VT4C = Verifiable, Traceable, Clear, Consistent, Complete, Concise.
  5. Interviews describe users' reports; Observation may reveal unreported behavior.
  6. User Story = As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit].
  7. MoSCoW priority differs from task dependencies; control Scope Creep.
  8. Requirements Definition feeds Models and forms part of System Proposal.
